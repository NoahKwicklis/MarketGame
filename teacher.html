Aggregate demand & supply — from the live roster

Every buyer’s units pooled and sorted high-to-low, every seller’s pooled and sorted low-to-high — the horizontal sum of the individual schedules. Where the staircases cross is p* and q*. The shaded band is the full range of market-clearing prices; the dashed line is its midpoint. A thick band means marginal units are nearly tied — widen the intercept bounds to sharpen it.

Transaction prices in sequence — by round

The n-th trade of each round at the price it executed, out to the last trade that round. Horizontal dashed line = p*; vertical dashed line = predicted q*, so trades falling to its right mean the market moved more units than theory says it should.

Market parameters

Buyer i: MWP = (a + αi) − b·Q  ·  Seller j: MC = (c + γj) + d·Q. Applying mid-round is a live shock — new trades use the new schedule instantly.

Trader heterogeneity. Each buyer draws αi ~ U[αmin, αmax] and each seller draws γj ~ U[γmin, γmax], once, on joining. Set all four to 0 for an identical-traders market.

Data & housekeeping

Hard clear deletes every trade, score, and player, ejects all connected students to the join screen, and restarts a fresh simulation under the same room code.

Average price of the k-th trade

Dot k averages the price of every trader’s k-th transaction that round (each trade counts once for the buyer’s sequence and once for the seller’s). Dashed line = theoretical equilibrium p* from current parameters.

Trades in real time — seconds into round

Each dot is a trade plotted at the moment it happened within its round.

Price histogram — by round, vs. theory

Distribution of transaction prices each round (rounds overlap in translucent color). Theory says trades should pile up at the dashed p*.

Quantity histogram — widgets per trader, vs. theory

How many widgets each trader moved in a round (a trade counts for both its buyer and its seller). Dashed line = theoretical q* per trader. Zero-trade bars are counted from the current roster.
